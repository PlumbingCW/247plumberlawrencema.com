How a Tankless Water Heater Works, and What It Means in Lawrence, MA

Heat on demand, no storage tank. Here is the mechanism and the catches.

Talk to a plumber
(855) 575-2890
Call Now

By the Lawrence 24/7 Plumbing team · Updated 2026

A tankless water heater in Lawrence, MA heats water only while a tap is open. When you turn on a faucet, a flow sensor detects the water, a burner or electric element switches on, and a heat exchanger warms the water as it passes through. There is no 40 or 50 gallon tank sitting hot all day.

That means a steady supply that does not run out in the middle of a shower, as long as the unit is sized for the demand. It also means different installation needs and a winter performance limit that matters in New England.

What happens in the 30 seconds after you open the tap

Every tankless unit follows the same four-part sequence, gas or electric.

1

Flow sensor trips

Water moving through the unit past a minimum flow rate tells the control board that someone wants hot water. Very low flow can fail to trip it.

2

Heat source fires

A gas unit lights a burner. An electric unit powers heating elements. Either one is controlled to match the water flow and temperature you want.

3

Heat exchanger transfers heat

Cold water passes through coils or plates heated by the burner or elements. The water gains heat in seconds as it moves through.

4

Thermostat adjusts output

Sensors watch the outlet temperature and tweak the firing rate. When you close the tap, the flow stops and the heater shuts off.

Parts worth knowing by name

When a technician says a part failed, this is what each one does.

Flow sensor

Detects water movement and starts the heating cycle.

Heat exchanger

Transfers heat from the burner or elements to the water. Scale can clog it.

Control board

Reads sensors, adjusts the heat, and displays error codes.

Venting and condensate line

Carries exhaust outdoors on gas units. High-efficiency models also drain acidic condensate, which needs a proper path.

Inlet filter

A small screen that catches debris before it reaches the exchanger.

Isolation valves

Let a plumber flush the unit with descaler without cutting water to the house.

Why Lawrence winters change the math

NOAA's 1991-2020 normals for the Lawrence station show a January average low of 19.6 F. In cold months, incoming water is often colder than in summer. A tankless heater has to lift that colder water to your set temperature, so the same unit can deliver less hot water per minute in January than in July.

So size the unit for winter, not summer. Tell your plumber your number of bathrooms, your showers that run at once, and whether you want a laundry or dishwasher on at the same time. A single unit that is sized for summer can leave a second shower lukewarm in February.

Tankless versus a storage tank

Neither is always better. The right choice depends on how you live.

Tankless strengths

  • Does not run out while the unit can keep up
  • No standby heat loss from a stored tank
  • Smaller wall-mounted footprint
  • Often a longer service life with maintenance

Tankless trade-offs

  • Higher install cost than a like-for-like tank swap
  • Winter flow rate can drop
  • Gas units may need bigger gas piping and special venting
  • Needs regular descaling in many homes

If your hot water use is low and your basement is tight, a tank may fit better. If you have a big household or an unused tank corner, tankless can make sense.

Gas, electric, and what installation requires

A gas tankless unit burns more gas per minute than a tank burner. Gas piping is sized under NFPA 54 as adopted in Massachusetts, so the existing line may need upgrading. Vent type and routing also change with the model.

Electric tankless units draw a large load and may need a dedicated circuit and a panel upgrade. A licensed electrician handles the electrical side while the plumber handles water and gas connections.

In Massachusetts, plumbing and gas fitting work needs a permit from the local inspector and a licensed professional. Replacing a vented gas appliance also requires a carbon monoxide detector on the same floor and each habitable level. Our tankless water heater page explains the options, and the water heater replacement page compares swapping like for like. Venting and gas sizing for a tankless unit follow the manufacturer's installation standards and the gas piping standards adopted in Massachusetts.

Questions to settle before you buy

Run through these with your plumber before you commit.

  • How many fixtures run at the same time in a busy morning?
  • Is my gas line big enough, or does it need upgrading?
  • Where will the vent exit the house?
  • Where will condensate drain?
  • Does my water leave scale, and should I add a softener or filter?
  • What is the maintenance plan, including yearly flushing?
  • Is a recirculation loop or buffer tank advisable?
