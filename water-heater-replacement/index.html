Water heater replacement in Lawrence, MA is usually forced by a leak, not planned over coffee. A tank that is rusting through cannot be patched, and a basement floor with an inch of water cannot wait until morning. Our 24/7 dispatch line takes these calls. Close the cold supply to the tank, cut the power or gas, and tell us the fuel type and where the water is showing.

A replacement means a new tank, correct sizing, a relief valve piped to code, and a permit. It also means a plain explanation of why the old one could not be saved.

A flooded basement and a dead heater: what to do right now

A water heater flood is not slow. A full tank holds 30 to 50 gallons, and once the shell opens the contents can reach a drain, a finished wall or a neighbor's ceiling in minutes. Shut the cold supply valve above the heater, kill the breaker or gas control, and keep power cords off the wet floor.

If you have no hot water and no puddle, the situation is calmer. Many dead heaters are a failed element or igniter, and our page on water heater repair covers those. Replacement enters the picture when the tank wall has failed or the repair would cost more than it saves.

Tonight's shutoff checklist

Do these in order. They protect the room while a plumber is on the way.

1

Stop the water

Close the cold-water valve on top of the heater. If it is stuck or leaking, shut the main at your meter instead.

2

Stop the heat

Turn off the electric breaker or switch the gas control to off. A heater with no water in it can burn out its element in seconds.

3

Move valuables and power

Lift stored boxes and electronics above the water line and keep cords off the floor. Avoid standing in water near an outlet.

4

Smell gas? Leave

Walk out, call 911 from outside and then Eversource Gas at (800) 525-8222. Do not call a plumber until the gas company says the building is safe.

Tell the dispatcher whether the old unit is gas, electric or oil, and how tall the closet or utility space is. That detail often decides which replacement tank can fit.

Choosing the right size for your household

Bigger is not automatically better. An oversized tank wastes energy keeping extra water hot, and one that is too small leaves the last person in line with a cold shower. Size depends on people in the home, the number of bathrooms and whether anyone runs the shower and the dishwasher together.

Apartments and two-families

Many Lawrence units sit in buildings of three or four units, and a shared heater has to cover several households at once. Ask the plumber to size for the busiest morning, not the average day, and to explain whether separate heaters per unit would make sense.

Tight utility closets

Basement corners and hall closets in older buildings often limit height and clearance. A short, wide tank or a heat pump unit with different clearance rules may fit where a standard tank will not. We measure before we quote.

What a proper replacement includes

A new tank is only part of the job. These are the pieces that make it safe and legal.

Relief valve and discharge pipe

Massachusetts code calls for a T&P relief valve with a full-size, unvalved discharge pipe ending within 12 inches of the floor.

Thermal expansion tank

Where a water meter or backflow device makes a closed system, code requires an expansion tank. Otherwise one is allowed and often a good idea.

Carbon monoxide detector

Replacing a vented gas appliance requires a CO detector on the same floor and on each habitable level.

Pan or leak shut-off

A safe waste pan or an approved automatic shut-off device is required in some installs, including units above living space.

Temperature setting

State code caps residential hot water at 130 degrees. The plumber sets and tests it before leaving.

Permit

Plumbing and gas fitting work that is replaced needs a permit from the local inspector. We pull it and arrange the inspection.

Heat pump water heaters and Mass Save

A heat pump water heater pulls warmth from the surrounding air and uses far less electricity than a standard electric tank. It needs room around it, a place for the condensate to drain, and usually a basement or large utility space that does not freeze.

Mass Save lists rebates for qualifying heat pump water heaters installed by a licensed plumber in 2026. Check the current amount and eligibility on masssave.com before you buy, since terms can change. Mass Save also says rebates on fossil-fuel storage water heaters are no longer available.

Hybrid units cost more up front. If your tank has already failed, you may not have time to shop, so ask the plumber for the standard option now and the heat pump option as a quote you can compare the next day.

Switching from gas to electric or keeping what you have

A like-for-like swap is the quickest path when the old heater is failing at night. Changing fuel takes more planning: a new circuit for electric, a vent capped for gas, and sometimes a panel upgrade.

If you rent out the unit, a fuel switch is worth asking about, since tenants benefit from reliable hot water and landlords benefit from fewer service calls. For a planned project, see our page on water heater installation. If you are weighing an on-demand unit, read about tankless water heaters.

Replacement in Lawrence's older buildings

About 53 percent of Lawrence housing was built in 1939 or earlier, and about 72 percent of homes are renter-occupied. Old basements often have low ceilings, cramped flues and shared utility spaces, so a plumber should look at the venting and clearance before quoting. Massachusetts sanitary code expects landlords to supply hot water at 110 to 130 degrees, which is why a failed heater in a rental calls for a same-day fix, not a long wait.

Permits go through Inspectional Services at 200 Common St, Room 209, 978-620-3130. Ask the local plumbing inspector about your specific job.

Questions about replacing a water heater in Lawrence

Yes. The dispatch line is open 24/7 and a failed tank is a call we take at any hour. Same-day appointments are offered when the schedule allows, and the dispatcher will walk you through shutting off water and power while a plumber is arranged.

Water coming through the tank wall means replacement. A leak at a fitting, a bad element or a tripped limit switch often means repair. Age matters too, but a plumber looks at the failure, not just the date.

A like-for-like swap commonly takes a few hours once the old tank is drained and the new one is on site. Fuel changes, venting work or a panel upgrade take longer, and the plumber will tell you before starting.
