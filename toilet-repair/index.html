Toilet repair in Lawrence, MA covers every fault between the bowl and the drain: an overflow, a leak at the base, a rocking seat on the floor, a tank that refills all night. When water is rising or soaking a floor, it is urgent, and our dispatch line runs 24/7 for exactly that.

While you wait, close the supply valve behind the toilet by turning it clockwise, lift the tank lid and hold the flapper down if the bowl is climbing, and put towels around the base. Do not flush again.

When an overflowing toilet is an emergency

A bowl that fills to the rim and keeps climbing is an emergency, especially in an apartment. Water that reaches the floor can travel through the subfloor to the ceiling of the unit below, and in a building with two to four units that is a second household and a second set of repairs.

Lawrence has a lot of that housing. The city's Housing Production Plan counts about 28 percent of units in 3-4 unit buildings and roughly 72 percent renter-occupied, so one bad toilet often affects more than one family. If you rent, tell the landlord the same hour. A new toilet or flange is set to the Massachusetts plumbing code, 248 CMR 10.00, and to the installation standards printed by the toilet's maker.

Overflow that will not stop

Shut the supply valve. If it is stuck, shut the unit's main water valve. A bowl that rises after one flush usually points to a blockage in the trap or drain line, so call before a third round with the plunger.

While you wait for the plumber

Two minutes of work can decide between a mop-up and a ceiling repair.

1

Close the supply valve

Turn the oval handle behind the toilet clockwise until it stops. Flush once to empty the tank only if the bowl is not already full.

2

Stop the fill

Lift the tank lid and raise the float or press the flapper closed so the bowl cannot take on more water.

3

Protect the floor

Lay towels around the base and move rugs out. Check the ceiling below if you share the building.

4

Keep people off the water

Keep children and pets out of an overflowed bathroom. If it came from a backed-up drain, treat it as dirty water and do not use other drains.

Signs your toilet needs repair

Some faults announce themselves. Others cost money quietly.

  • The tank refills every few minutes or you hear a faint hiss with nobody using it, which often means a worn flapper or fill valve.
  • The bowl is slow to empty or gurgles, which can point to a partial clog or a venting problem.
  • The toilet rocks when you sit, which suggests a loose flange or closet bolts.
  • The handle must be held down or jiggled to flush.
  • A water bill that creeps up with no change in use, since a running toilet can waste water silently.

How the fault is found

A licensed plumber works through the fixture in a set order.

1

Dye test the tank

A few drops of dye in the tank show whether the flapper is leaking into the bowl without a flush.

2

Check the water level and fill valve

The plumber compares the water line to the overflow tube and watches how the fill valve shuts off.

3

Rock-test the base

Any movement at the floor means the flange or bolts are loose. Water stains at the base point to the seal.

4

Camera or auger the drain if needed

If the bowl backs up, the plumber augers the toilet trap and, when the clog is further down, switches to drain-line tools.

Wax rings, flanges and fill valves

The wax ring seals the toilet outlet to the drain flange. It rarely fails alone. Rocking, a settling floor or a badly set flange usually comes first, which is why we check the flange before resetting.

Fill valves and flappers cause most running toilets. They are cheap, but the replacement should fit the tank model. Replace the shutoff valve and supply line while the toilet is out, since an old valve that will not close is a problem waiting for the next leak.

Why Lawrence buildings change the repair

Housing in Lawrence skews old. The city's Housing Production Plan reports about 53 percent of units built in 1939 or earlier, so ask the plumber to look at the drain under the toilet, not just the fixture.

In heavy rain, combined sewers along the Merrimack and Spicket Rivers can overload. If several fixtures back up at once, the cause may be the sewer lateral, which the property owner owns. Then the job moves to drain cleaning or sewer work, and it is better to know that on the first visit.

Permits and who may do the work

Massachusetts requires plumbing work in a home to be done by a licensed plumber under a permit from the local inspector, except minor leak repairs and clearing a clog. Resetting or replacing a toilet is not a clog, so ask the plumbing inspector at Lawrence Inspectional Services, 978-620-3130, how your job is classified.

A leak that only appears after a flush often means a failing wax ring or a loose bolt at the base. It can also be a cracked flange. A plumber checks all of them in one visit.

A cracked bowl or tank means replacement. A running tank, a loose seat or a wax ring leak is usually a repair. A plumber should show you the fault before suggesting a new fixture.
