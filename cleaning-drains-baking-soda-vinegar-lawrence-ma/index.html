Cleaning a Drain With Baking Soda and Vinegar in Lawrence, MA

A safe kitchen-sink method for slow drains, plus the honest limits of the trick.

Talk to a plumber
(866) 310-6010
Call Now

By the Lawrence 24/7 Plumbing team · Updated 2026

Cleaning drains with baking soda and vinegar in Lawrence, MA can help when a sink or tub drains slowly because of a light film of grease, soap and hair near the opening. The fizz loosens that residue and hot water carries it off. It will not move a solid blockage, tree roots, or a backup that shows up in more than one fixture.

Below are the exact steps, a quick guide to which drains respond, and the signs that tell you to stop and call a licensed plumber.

The five-minute method for a slow sink or tub

Use this on a drain that still moves water, only slowly. A drain that holds standing water for hours needs a different approach.

1

Clear the opening

Pull out the stopper or strainer and remove the hair and gunk you can see. Wipe it away with a paper towel and bag it. Do not rinse it down.

2

Flush with hot water

Run the hot tap for a minute, or pour in a kettle of hot water that has stopped boiling. Rolling boiling water can crack old porcelain and soften some plastic pipe.

3

Add the baking soda and vinegar

Pour in about half a cup of baking soda, then half a cup of white vinegar. Plug the opening loosely with a stopper so the fizz pushes down into the pipe rather than up at you.

4

Wait, then flush again

Give it 15 to 30 minutes. Then run hot water for a full minute and watch the speed. If it drains faster, repeat once more the next day if needed.

What the fizz really does inside the pipe

Baking soda and vinegar react to make carbon dioxide, water and a mild salt. The bubbling gives you a little agitation, and the baking soda is slightly abrasive. That is enough to loosen the thin, slick layer of soap scum and grease that builds on the walls of a trap.

It is not strong enough to dissolve a hairball the size of a fist, a wad of paper, or a child's toy. It also does nothing about a pipe that has sagged, cracked or filled with roots. Think of it as light maintenance, not repair. According to the Massachusetts plumbing board, clearing a clog is one of the few jobs that does not need a permit, which is why a home remedy is a fair first step.

Signs to stop and call a plumber

Repeating the fizz for days wastes time and can hide a real problem. Stop if you see any of these.

  • Water sits in the sink or tub for hours and barely moves.
  • Two or more fixtures slow down together, such as the kitchen sink and the washing machine drain.
  • You hear gurgling in a nearby drain when you run water elsewhere.
  • A toilet bubbles or water rises in a floor drain when you use the shower.
  • The same drain slows again within a week of clearing it.
  • A sewer smell lingers near a basement floor drain or a cleanout.
  • You rent the unit and the drain is shared with other apartments.

Any of these suggests the blockage sits deeper in the line than a household method can reach. Our drain cleaning service uses cable machines and, for heavy buildup, hydro jetting to clear the pipe wall instead of poking a hole through the clog.

Safety rules for any DIY drain clearing

Never pour a store-bought chemical drain opener into a drain and then add vinegar or bleach. Mixing cleaners can release harmful fumes. If you already used a chemical product and the drain is still blocked, tell the plumber what you used so they can wear the right gloves and eye protection.

Do not use baking soda and vinegar in a drain that is fully blocked and holding water. The mixture just sits there and the water will not carry it down. Use a plunger or a hand auger on the trap, or call for help.

Who owns which pipe in Lawrence

The City of Lawrence maintains 137 miles of sanitary and combined sewer mains. The pipe that runs from your house to that main is called the lateral, and the property owner owns it. That means a root or grease blockage in the lateral is your repair, not the city's.

So if the whole building backs up, or a floor drain in a basement gurgles after a fizz treatment, treat it as a lateral question and ask a plumber to camera the line. If you think the problem is in the street main, call Lawrence Water & Sewer at 978-620-3110.

Keeping a drain clear without chemicals

A cheap hair catcher in every tub and shower stops most bathroom clogs before they start. In the kitchen, wipe greasy pans with a paper towel and put the fat in the trash, not the sink. Garbage disposals grind food but do not stop grease from coating the pipe past them.

A monthly hot water flush and a baking soda and vinegar treatment can keep light film from building in a trap. Households in Methuen and North Andover with older cast iron or galvanized lines may notice buildup sooner, because rough pipe walls hold onto residue. If one drain keeps slowing, our plumbers serve Methuen and North Andover as well as Lawrence and can look at the line.
