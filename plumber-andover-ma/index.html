Looking for an emergency plumber in Andover, MA usually means something has gone wrong with water: a flooded cellar, a failed tank, a pipe that let go, or a drain that backed up. Our 24/7 dispatch line covers the town from the Lawrence side, roughly four miles from Town Hall.

Andover plumbing has a few features worth knowing before anyone opens a wall. The town publishes detailed water results, it is removing lead service lines, and it bans sump water from its sewer.

What the town's water report says about your pipes

Andover's Water and Sewer Division draws mostly from Haggetts Pond, with Fish Brook and the Merrimack River as supplements, and treats it at a plant on Lowell Street. Its 2023 report lists hardness at 38.5 mg/L, which the town calls soft, and a pH of 8.88.

Soft water tends to leave less scale than hard water, so a tank water heater may collect less crust. Sediment still builds at the bottom over time, though, so a flush and an anode check remain worth asking about. Our water heater repair calls are a good time to raise it.

The higher pH is there for corrosion control. If you see blue-green staining or pinhole leaks in copper, mention your water results on the call so the plumber can weigh them.

Lead lines: where Andover stands and what to ask

Under a consent order signed in April 2022, the town is removing lead service lines and investigating lines of unknown material. It took out 45 lead service lines in 2023. Lead at the 90th percentile measured 13.8 ppb that year, against a 15 ppb action level, in samples drawn from homes known to have lead lines or lead solder.

What that means for you is practical. Before a plumber replaces a water line or meter connection, ask the Water and Sewer Division at (978) 623-8700 what material the town has on record and where the town's responsibility ends. A leaking lead service is usually replaced, not patched, and the town's program may affect timing and cost. Our water line repair crews can work around that conversation.

Sump pumps and the sewer rule

Andover's sewer regulations prohibit rain water, sump pump water and surface runoff from entering the sanitary sewer. A sump that discharges into a floor drain or a sewer cleanout breaks that rule, and the fix is a discharge line that goes outdoors.

A good setup also has an accessible check valve on the discharge piping, as the state plumbing code requires for ejector and sump discharge. Our sump pump work covers replacement, check valves and battery backup. If water keeps returning, a basement water control review looks at the grading and drains around the foundation.

Sewer or septic: know which your house has

Most Andover wastewater goes to the Greater Lawrence Sanitary District, but the town's housing plan says some properties use on-site septic systems. Which streets are sewered is not something we assume.

The answer changes the call. A sewered house with a blocked lateral needs sewer line repair or cleaning. A septic house with the same symptoms may have a full tank or failed leach field, which is a different trade. Ask the town or look for a cleanout and a tank lid before you describe the problem.

Older houses in Shawsheen Village and beyond

Over a third of Andover's housing, about 37 percent, was built before 1960. Shawsheen Village was laid out in 1919 and the early 1920s, so some buildings there are a century old.

That age means a plumber may find mixed materials, from old iron drains to later copper and plastic repairs. Ask for a camera look at any drain you plan to keep, and expect part-replacement discussions rather than single fixes. A repiping conversation is reasonable when the same house has leaks in more than one spot.

Gas smells and the 2018 memory

On September 13, 2018, a gas overpressurization in the Merrimack Valley led Andover crews to put out 38 fires and respond to 17 more gas leaks. Many residents still treat any odor seriously, and they are right to.

If you smell gas, leave the house, call 911 from outside, avoid switches, then call the gas company. Eversource now operates the former Columbia Gas system. Once the gas is cleared, we can look at the piping or appliance.

While Dispatch Gets a Plumber to You

Andover's utilities offer a few extra levers worth knowing.

1

Find the curb stop and meter valve

Shutting the valve at the meter stops the house supply. Ask the Water and Sewer Division before touching the curb stop in the yard.

2

Unplug the sump, not the panel

If a flooded sump is near power, disconnect only the pump cord when the floor is dry. Leave the breaker alone if you are standing in water.

3

Stop sending water down

No dishwasher, no laundry, no flushing while a drain is backing up. Every gallon you add finds the lowest opening.

4

Write down what you saw

Note the time, which fixtures were involved and any smell or discoloration. Dispatch and your insurer will both ask.
