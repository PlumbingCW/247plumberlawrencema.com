Know when to call

What Counts as a Plumbing Emergency

A real emergency is water or waste that keeps spreading, or the loss of something a household cannot do without.

  • Water you cannot stop. A split pipe, a failed supply line or a tank that keeps emptying adds water until someone closes the main valve.
  • Sewage coming up. Wastewater in a floor drain, tub or basement is a health problem, not only a clog.
  • No water at all. The state sanitary code treats a home without enough water for 24 hours or more as a danger to health and safety.
  • A water heater leaking from the tank body. A drip at a fitting can wait for a booked visit. A tank that is emptying onto the floor cannot.
  • Water coming through a ceiling or wall. Bulging drywall or water near a light fixture calls for a fast shutoff and a fast call.
  • A frozen pipe that has split. Ice can hide a crack until the pipe thaws and the water starts flowing.

A gas smell is different. Leave the building, call 911 from outside, then call your gas utility. Call a plumber only after the gas company says the home is safe.

Slow drains, dripping faucets and running toilets usually fit a same-day appointment when one is available.

While you wait

Before the Plumber Gets There

Ten minutes of the right steps can save a ceiling. Tell the dispatcher what you finished.

1

Find and close the main shutoff

In many older homes it sits where the water line enters the basement, often near the meter. Turn it clockwise until it stops, then open a low faucet to drain the lines.

2

Be careful near power

If the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water, switch off breakers for wet areas. If not, stay out and say so on the call.

3

Move and photograph

Lift rugs, boxes and electronics off the floor, set out buckets and take photos of the damage for your insurance claim.

4

Clear a path

Open the way to the shutoff, the water heater and the affected drain. In a multi-unit building, unlock the basement door and tell neighbors.

Who owns what

Which Pipe Belongs to You and Which to the City

Lawrence Water & Sewer rules say the city repairs the water line from the main to the curb stop and the owner is responsible from there on. The city web page words it as running up to the meter, so confirm the dividing point at 978-620-3110 before paying for a dig.

The sewer lateral, the pipe from your house to the street main, belongs to the property owner. If neighbors are backing up too, report it to the city overflow line at 978-620-3580. If only your drains are slow, a camera view shows which side the blockage is on.
