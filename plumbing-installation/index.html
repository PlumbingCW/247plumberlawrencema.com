Plumbing installation in Lawrence, MA means putting in new water, drain and gas-fitting connections so a fixture, room or building works the first time. Most jobs start with a plan on paper, such as a vanity swap, a second bathroom in a triple-decker, or a rough-in for an addition.

A licensed plumber walks the space, checks existing pipes and drains, and gives you a price before work begins.

Rough-in: where the pipes go before the walls close

Rough-in is the pipework inside the walls and floor before drywall and tile. Supply lines, drains, vents and valve bodies must be in the right place because moving them later means opening finished surfaces.

A plumber should settle fixture locations, drain slopes and vent paths before framing is closed. In a bathroom, that includes the tub or shower valve height, the toilet flange distance from the wall, and where the vanity supply stubs come out.

If you want a fixture moved, ask for the drain run to be checked first. A toilet cannot go just anywhere, because it needs a drain size and a vent in reach. New plumbing follows the Massachusetts plumbing code, 248 CMR 10.00, and the standards your local plumbing inspector applies at inspection.

Bathroom rough-in

Includes the valve, shower drain, toilet flange and sink stubs, plus venting. A new bathroom added over a living space also needs a drain that can reach the stack.

Kitchen rough-in

Covers the sink supply and drain, dishwasher connection and, if planned, a gas line for a range. Gas work is performed by a licensed gas fitter under a gas permit.

Installing in Lawrence's mill and multifamily buildings

Lawrence has about 12 million square feet of mill space, and its Housing Production Plan notes mill overlay districts for reuse. Adaptive reuse means new apartments and offices need new risers, drains and fixtures, so ask early how the plumbing will connect to the building's existing stack.

Housing here also runs multifamily: about 28 percent of units sit in 3-4 unit buildings and about 12 percent in two-unit buildings. A new bathroom in one unit affects the drain shared with the rest, which is why we check capacity and venting before we cut.

Permits and inspections

Massachusetts requires a permit from the local inspector before plumbing and gas fitting work that is installed, altered, removed, replaced or repaired. Only minor leak repairs and clearing a clog are exempt. There is no homeowner DIY exemption in the Board's fact sheet, so a licensed plumber needs to do the work.

In Lawrence, ask Inspectional Services, 200 Common St, Room 209, 978-620-3130, or use the online permit portal. The inspector must respond to an inspection request within two working days.

New construction plumbing

New-build plumbing goes in stages. The underground drain and water service go in first, then rough-in after framing, then fixtures at the end. Exterior water lines need cover below the state frost depth of 48 inches.

Plan fixture counts, a main shutoff location and the water heater space with the builder before framing. For a connection to city water or sewer, confirm requirements with Lawrence Water & Sewer, 978-620-3110. The owner owns the sewer lateral, so it is worth a camera check before connecting a new drain, see sewer line repair.
