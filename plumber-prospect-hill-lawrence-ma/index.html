The City of Lawrence lists a Prospect Hill and Back Bay neighborhood association. It meets at the Rollins School on Howard Street on the third Wednesday of most months, so that is the name many residents use for the area.

An older house is not a problem by itself. It is a reason to learn what pipes you have before something fails, so you can choose between repair and replacement on your own schedule instead of during a leak.

What we know about Prospect Hill and what we do not

The city housing plan says portions of the Prospect Hill neighborhood fall inside historic districts. The water department lists the Prospect Hill Tank among the places the city stores finished water. Data and listing sites place Marston, Prospect, Ferry, High and East Streets in or near the area, but the boundaries differ from one source to the next.

We do not claim to know what pipes any house on the hill contains. No one has published a pipe survey by neighborhood, and two houses on the same street can have very different plumbing. The steps below show how to find out for your own address.

How to find out what your pipes are made of

You can learn a lot in ten minutes with a flashlight.

1

Look where the water enters

Find the pipe near the meter. Copper is reddish brown and turns green with age. Galvanized steel is gray and a magnet sticks to it. Plastic is white, cream or colored.

2

Check the city's map

Lawrence publishes a service line inventory map. If your line shows as lead or unknown, ask Lawrence Water and Sewer at 978-620-3110 what the city has on record.

3

Look at the drains

Dark, heavy pipe that rings when tapped is usually cast iron. Smooth white or black pipe is plastic. Note any rust stains at joints.

4

Write it down and call

Pictures and notes let a plumber suggest a sensible next step without guessing.

Water heater swaps, permits and the vent

In Massachusetts, plumbing work that replaces or repairs a fixture or heater needs a permit from the local inspector first (248 CMR 3.05). Only minor leak repairs and clearing a clog are permit-free. Lawrence Inspectional Services is at 978-620-3130, and the Board of State Examiners of Plumbers and Gas Fitters sets the rules.

A new tank needs a temperature and pressure relief valve with a full-size discharge pipe that ends within 12 inches of the floor. State code caps residential hot water at 130 F (248 CMR 10.14). If the old heater is a vented gas appliance, a carbon monoxide detector is required on the same floor and on each habitable level.

Our water heater replacement and water heater installation pages walk through the decision. Mass Save lists rebates for qualifying heat pump water heaters, so check masssave.com for the current amounts.

Kitchens, baths and the repipe question

Remodeling an older kitchen or bath often means new supply lines, new drains and a new set of valves. It is the easiest time to replace anything doubtful, because the wall is already open. A plumber can price plumbing installation work alongside the carpentry.

Repiping is a bigger step. It makes sense when leaks keep appearing in different places, when pipes are failing inside the walls or when water quality problems trace back to the pipe itself. One pinhole leak alone does not call for it. A repiping decision should rest on what you found at the meter and in the basement. For a dripping tap or a loose handle, faucet and sink repair is usually the better first step.

Questions to ask any plumber about an older house

These protect you whoever you hire.

  • Is the plumber's license number on the quote? Check it on the Mass.gov license lookup.
  • Who pulls the permit and who schedules the inspection?
  • What is the plan if the wall turns out to hold a different pipe than expected?
  • Will old shutoff valves be replaced while the water is off?
  • What exactly is covered if the same leak returns?
