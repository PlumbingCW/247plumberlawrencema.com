What a North Andover address tells us before we arrive

North Andover is a town in Essex County with a 2020 population of 30,915 and one ZIP, 01845. Its drinking water comes from Lake Cochichewick, a 560-acre lake that supplies about 28,000 residents. We do not quote a hardness or water quality number for the town, because we have not verified one. If scale or staining worries you, test your own tap.

Sewage in the part of town on the public system travels to the Greater Lawrence Sanitary District plant, which sits inside North Andover itself. So when we ask whether your house is on town sewer or a septic tank, the answer decides which pipe we inspect first.

Town sewer along Route 125, or a septic tank out back

The town extended sewer about 11,000 feet along Route 125 between 2020 and December 2023, partly to replace septic systems. Some homes in that corridor may have switched from a tank to a town connection. If yours did, an old tank or its pipework may still sit in the yard, so ask the seller or the town what was abandoned and how.

A slow main drain is a different job depending on the answer. On a sewer connection, a stoppage is usually in the lateral or the house line, and we start with drain cleaning and a camera if it repeats.

On septic, a backup can mean a full tank or a failing leach field, and that is a septic contractor's work, not ours. We will tell you which one you have before we charge you for the wrong fix. Work in North Andover follows the same Massachusetts plumbing code, 248 CMR 10.00, that the town plumbing inspector enforces.

Selling a home on septic

Massachusetts requires a Title 5 septic inspection within two years before a sale. That inspection is separate from a plumbing check, so ask your agent which comes first. If the inspector or buyer flags a line to the house, a sewer line repair visit can sort out whether the problem is the tank or the pipe.

Four corners of town, four kinds of calls

The town runs a Watershed Protection Overlay District with special-permit rules for building there. Before digging up a water or sewer line near the lake, ask the town what applies.

Before We Arrive at Your North Andover Home

A few minutes of work from you can save the finished floor while the plumber drives over.

1

Find and close the main

Look near where the water line enters the basement or utility room. Turn the valve clockwise until it stops, then open a low tap to drain the pipes.

2

Cut power near wet floors

If water is touching outlets, a basement panel or an electric heater, stay out of the water and shut off the breaker only if you can reach it dry.

3

Clear the way

Move rugs, boxes and stored items away from the drain, the heater and the wall that is wet. A clear floor lets the work begin faster.

4

Photograph and call

Take two or three photos for your insurer, then call the 24/7 line and tell dispatch if you are on town sewer, on septic, or unsure.

If you smell gas, leave the building, call 911 from outside, and then call your gas company. Plumbing can wait until the gas is cleared.
