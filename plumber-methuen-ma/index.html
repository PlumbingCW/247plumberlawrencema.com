The calls that come in from Methuen houses

Methuen has single-family streets, multi-family buildings and older homes near the Spicket River. The same handful of problems repeat.

A drain that will not move

Kitchen sinks, tubs and basement floor drains. Water sits or climbs instead of leaving. Cabling or jetting usually clears it.

Sewage in the lowest drain

Gurgling toilets and water in the basement floor drain point to the main line. That is a same-day problem in most houses.

Stains under a bathroom

A brown ring on the ceiling below a tub or toilet often means a slow leak in a supply or drain joint.

A meter that will not stop

When the dial spins with every fixture off, water is escaping somewhere between the meter and the taps.

A split or frozen pipe

Cold snaps open seams in pipes along exterior walls, garages and unheated crawl spaces.

Cold showers

A tank that leaks from the bottom is done. One that only stopped heating often needs a part.

Each of those has its own section below, in the order Methuen callers tend to need them.

Slow sinks, backups and the lowest drain in the house

Most drain calls start small. One tub drains slowly, then the toilet gurgles when the washer empties. By the time water shows in a basement floor drain, the blockage is usually in the main line and not in a fixture.

A single clogged fixture is handled with drain cleaning, which means cabling the trap arm or the branch line. When several fixtures act up together, the cleanout needs sewer line cleaning, often with a camera pass afterward to see why it clogged.

Why the main line is the usual suspect

Roots, grease and a bellied or cracked section can all catch debris. Older clay and cast iron laterals commonly have joints where roots enter. The lateral from your house to the street sewer belongs to the owner, so ask for a camera look before agreeing to a dig.

A ceiling stain means find the source before you patch

Water shows up far from where it starts. A stain in a Methuen kitchen ceiling can come from the bathroom above, from a tub drain, or from a supply line running through the floor joists.

Leak detection uses moisture meters, thermal imaging and listening equipment to trace it without opening every wall. If the water meter turns with everything shut off, say so on the call. It tells the plumber to look at the service line and hidden supply runs first.

When a pipe splits or a heater quits

A frozen pipe often hides until the thaw. Then the seam opens and water pours into a wall or floor. Burst pipe repair starts by finding where the pipe cracked, cutting out the damaged run and replacing it with sound pipe, not a clamp.

Hot water failures split two ways. A tank that drips from the base cannot be patched, so the choice becomes replacement. A tank with no heat but no puddle often needs an element, thermostat or burner part, which is a job for water heater repair. A call to the line sorts out which one you have.

No water at all is sometimes the city's problem

Methuen has had large main breaks. One on Oakland Avenue in June 2022 left more than 100 homes without water, and one on East Street in May 2023 affected about 30. If every tap goes dry together, ask a neighbor before you call a plumber.

If the whole street is out, the problem sits in the city main, and the Methuen Water Department runs a 24-hour distribution emergency line at 978-983-8855. If only your house is dry, check the main shutoff and the meter valve, then call us.

Wet basements near the Spicket and Merrimack

In May 2006 the Merrimack and Spicket Rivers rose over their banks and hundreds of Methuen residents were evacuated. Low basements near either river can still take water after heavy rain, even without a flood.

A working sump pump with a check valve on the discharge is the first line of defense, and a battery backup helps when storms knock out power. Standard homeowner policies often exclude sump failure and sewer backup unless an endorsement is added, so ask your agent before you need it.

What a Methuen job looks like on paper

Methuen is one ZIP, 01844. Drinking water comes from the Merrimack River through the city filtration plant. The city says it has no known lead service lines, though older homes may still have galvanized pipe inside, which is a reason to check before assuming the pipes are copper.

Sewage leaves through the city collection system to the Greater Lawrence Sanitary District plant in North Andover. About a quarter of Methuen homes were built in 1939 or earlier, and roughly 4 in 10 before 1960, so a plumber should expect old drain materials and mixed repairs.

Permits go through the Inspections Division at 41 Pleasant St, Suite 203, in the Searles Building. Plumbing inspector line is 978-983-8634. Ask us to explain what needs a permit before work begins.

Before We Arrive at Your Methuen Home

These steps limit damage while the line gets a plumber headed your way.

1

Close the main valve

Find the shutoff where the water line enters, often by the meter in a basement. Turn it clockwise until it stops.

2

Cut power near the water

If water reaches outlets, appliances or the panel, switch off that breaker only if the floor around it is dry.

3

Move what can be moved

Lift rugs, boxes and furniture off wet floors. Keep people away from any room with a sagging ceiling.

4

Call with specifics

Tell dispatch the room, what you see and what you already shut off. Photos help when the plumber walks in.

Smell gas? Leave the building, call 911 from outside, then call the gas company before anyone else.
