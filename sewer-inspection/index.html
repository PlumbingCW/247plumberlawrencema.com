A sewer camera inspection in Lawrence, MA sends a small waterproof camera through the pipe that carries waste from your house to the city sewer. A technician watches the live video, records it, and explains what the pipe looks like inside. It is the quickest way to learn why drains behave badly or what you are buying.

You do not need a crisis to book one. Most inspections happen because of a slow drain that keeps coming back, a smell, or a home sale. If water is already rising in your basement, call our sewer line cleaning team first.

Gurgling drains, sewer smells and backups that keep returning

You have had the line cleared, and it still gurgles. A faint sewer smell comes and goes in the basement. A toilet makes noise when the washer drains. These signs usually mean something is wrong inside the main drain or the lateral, and a plunger will not find it.

The tool that shows the problem has a technical name: sewer camera inspection, sometimes called a sewer scope. The camera tells you if the cause is grease, roots, a crack, a sag or a joint that has shifted.

How the inspection works

A typical visit takes a few steps. The details depend on access.

1

Find an entry point

The camera goes in through a cleanout cap, a pulled toilet or an access point on the main.

2

Run the camera

The technician feeds the camera on a flexible rod while a monitor shows the live picture.

3

Note and record

Depth marks, cracks, roots and sags are noted. Footage can usually be saved.

4

Explain the result

You get a plain explanation of what the pipe shows and which next steps make sense.

What the footage shows

Each finding means something different for your decision.

Roots

Fine hairs or thick masses at a joint. Roots mean there is an opening, so cutting them is temporary.

Cracks and breaks

Lines running along or across the pipe. A hairline may be monitored. A break needs repair.

Offset joints

Sections that no longer line up. Debris catches here and backups often follow.

Bellies (sags)

Low spots where water pools after the flow passes. They can hold waste and clog repeatedly.

Scale and grease

Rough buildup narrows the pipe. Jetting may clean it if the pipe is sound.

Collapse or blockage

A section that has caved in or is full of debris. This usually needs sewer line repair.

Buying a house with an old sewer line

A standard home inspection does not usually run a camera through the lateral. If you are buying in Lawrence, you can ask for a sewer scope before the inspection period ends. The city's housing plan reports that about 53 percent of units were built in 1939 or earlier, so many laterals are old.

In Lawrence the property owner owns the lateral, which means a bad pipe becomes your repair after closing. Ask for the footage and a written note of what the technician saw, and bring it to the purchase negotiation. Confirm the owner's boundary with Lawrence Water & Sewer at 978-620-3110.

Buying in a town with septic

Some properties in Andover and North Andover use on-site septic, and Massachusetts requires a Title 5 inspection within two years before a sale. A sewer camera is not a septic inspection. Check which system the house has. See North Andover for local notes.

Locating the line, depth and cleanout

A locator is a handheld receiver that picks up a signal from the camera head. The technician can mark the surface above the camera and read the depth. That tells you where the pipe runs under the lawn, driveway or sidewalk, and how deep a repair would have to be.

Locating also helps you avoid surprises. If the line runs beneath a garage slab, under a porch or across a neighbor's lot, you want to know before anyone plans a dig. The mark is temporary. Take photos or ask for a sketch.

Frost depth in Massachusetts is 48 inches, according to the state building code. Shallow laterals can be affected by freezing in extreme cold, so depth readings matter for insulation and repair choices.

What a camera inspection cannot tell you

A camera sees only the section it reaches. If the lateral is blocked, it may need cleaning first. If the problem is in the city main under the street, the camera on your side cannot fix it. In that case, report it on the sanitary overflow line at 978-620-3580 or 978-620-3110.

It also cannot see through walls or measure soil support. A good technician says where the picture ends and does not guess beyond it. You can also share the footage with an independent plumber for a second opinion.

When it makes sense to schedule a camera inspection

You do not need a camera for every slow drain. These are the situations where it pays.

  • The main drain has been cleared more than once in a year.
  • A sewer smell comes from a floor drain or the basement.
  • You are buying or selling a home.
  • A large tree grows close to the lateral.
  • You plan an addition or a basement bathroom that will add load to the line.
  • The cable returns with clay chips, rust or roots.
  • You want a baseline before a repair or lining job.
