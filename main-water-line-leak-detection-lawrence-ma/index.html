Main Water Line Leak Detection in Lawrence, MA: How to Find It

A leaking main line rarely shows itself. Here is how to confirm it with your own meter before anyone digs.

By the Lawrence 24/7 Plumbing team · Updated 2026

Main water line leak detection in Lawrence, MA starts at your water meter. The main line is the buried pipe that carries water from the street into your home, and when it leaks, the water often vanishes into soil instead of showing up as a puddle.

The giveaway is a meter that keeps moving when every tap, toilet and appliance is off. You can check that in about ten minutes with no tools. The sections below cover the test, what the result means, who owns which stretch of pipe, and when it is time to call a licensed plumber.

Signs the line under your yard or floor is losing water

One sign alone can have a harmless cause. Two or three together make a buried leak worth testing for.

  • The water bill rises for a month or two with no change in how many people live in the home or how much laundry gets done.
  • The meter dial or digital flow indicator keeps moving after you shut off every fixture inside.
  • A patch of lawn stays soft, green or sunken while the rest of the yard dries out.
  • A basement wall near where the pipe enters the house feels damp, or the floor there stays cool and wet.
  • Water pressure drops through the whole house at once, not at one faucet.

The ten-minute meter test

Do this when nobody is using water, such as before the morning shower rush.

1

Shut off everything

Turn off all taps, the dishwasher, the washing machine and the ice maker. Check that no toilet is refilling. If you have a softener or an irrigation system, make sure it is not cycling.

2

Read the meter

Open the meter lid or look at the display. Note the numbers and watch the small leak indicator, often a triangle or star, for any spin. Write the reading down or take a photo.

3

Wait ten minutes

Keep every fixture off. Do not flush. A longer wait, such as an hour, makes a slow leak easier to see.

4

Compare the readings

If the numbers moved or the indicator kept spinning, water is going somewhere. If nothing changed, the main line is probably tight and the bill has another cause.

What your meter result points to

The meter shows that water is moving, not where it is going. Use the result to pick the next step.

What you seeWhat it often meansNext step
Meter moves, one toilet is runningA worn flapper or fill valve insideShut the toilet supply and retest
Meter moves with the house valve closedLeak is on the street side of your main shutoffCall Lawrence Water & Sewer first, then a plumber
Meter stops with the house valve closed, moves when openLeak is inside or under the home, on the house sideCall a licensed plumber for leak detection

Closing the main shutoff valve is the key split. It separates the pipe in your walls from the pipe in your yard.

Who owns which stretch of pipe in Lawrence

Lawrence Water Division rules say the city repairs the service from the main in the street to the curb stop, and the owner is responsible from the curb stop onward. The city web page words it as "up to and including your meter," which does not match, so confirm the exact dividing point with Lawrence Water & Sewer at 978-620-3110 before you pay for any repair.

Material matters too. The city's 2025 water quality report counts 12,777 service lines, with up to 57 lead lines possibly remaining and 345 of unknown material. Since January 2014 new lead cannot be introduced into a water service, so a lead line cannot be patched, only replaced. If your leak is on an old line, ask the city which material it shows for your address on its public service line inventory map.

How a plumber pinpoints a leak without tearing up the yard

Once the meter test says water is escaping, the job is locating it. Licensed plumbers commonly combine a few methods rather than relying on one. Each narrows the dig to a few feet instead of the whole front lawn.

Electronic listening gear picks up the sound of water escaping from a pressurized pipe, and a ground microphone helps mark the loudest spot. A pressure test isolates sections of pipe to show which stretch loses pressure. Thermal imaging can reveal a warm trail along a hot line under a floor. According to Massachusetts guidance, the water supplier and the homeowner each have their own side of the service line, so confirm the boundary before you dig.

Winter, frost and the buried line

The state building code sets frost depth at 48 inches, and the Lawrence station's January average low is about 19.6 F. A service line laid shallower than that can freeze and crack, and the break may only show once the ground warms.

The city's rules say it takes no responsibility for frozen service lines, so a winter break usually falls to the owner. The city's own guidance is to keep the thermostat at 55 F or higher and insulate pipes near the entry point.

What shapes the cost of finding and fixing a main line leak

No honest number exists until someone knows where the leak is. These are the factors that move the total.

Where the leak sitsA leak on the owner's side near the meter is a smaller job than one under a driveway, a sidewalk or a slab.
Pipe material and ageCopper, galvanized steel, plastic and lead behave differently and call for different repairs. A lead line has to be replaced, not patched.
Length of the damaged runA short section can be spliced. A badly corroded line may be cheaper to replace than to chase leak after leak.
Digging versus trenchless optionsHand digging a small hole costs less than opening a long trench. Some lines allow less disruptive methods and others do not.
Restoration after the repairPatching pavement, lawn or flooring is often a separate line on the job.
Home insurance often covers sudden and accidental water damage, but the cost of repairing the pipe itself and slow leaks that go on for weeks are handled differently by each policy. Ask your agent before work starts.
