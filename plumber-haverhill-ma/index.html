A river city drains differently when it rains

Haverhill sits on the Merrimack, and part of its sewer system is old combined pipe that carries sewage and stormwater together. The city reports about 1,500 acres of combined sewers and 13 overflow outfalls. A heavy storm can fill those pipes quickly, and the lowest drain in a basement can be the first place the water shows up.

The city's own sewer page notes that roof drains, sump pumps and foundation drains add flow to combined sewers in wet weather. If water backs up through a floor drain during a storm, shut the washer off and keep fixtures still. Then call us, so we can tell a blocked house line from a street-side surge, and ask whether a backwater valve fits your pipe layout. Check with the sewer authority before installing one.

When the problem is in your pipe, not the street

A single fixture that backs up while the rest drain normally often points to a stoppage in your own line. Our drain cleaning visit starts there. If the main line backs up again within weeks, a camera run shows whether roots or a collapsed section are the cause.

January freeze versus a spring downpour

Haverhill's two worst plumbing seasons call for different responses.

Deep cold

  • The state code puts frost depth at 48 inches, and the Lawrence weather station averages a January low near 19.6 F.
  • Pipes in an unheated garage, porch or exterior wall are the usual suspects.
  • Keep the heat on and open cabinet doors under sinks on cold nights.
  • A split pipe often shows itself only after the thaw. Close the main and call about burst pipe repair.

Storm and thaw

  • Annual precipitation at the Lawrence weather station runs roughly 41 to 49 inches.
  • Combined sewers and wet basements are the usual trouble spots.
  • Sump pumps work hardest during snowmelt and long rain.
  • Test the pump before the storm. A sump pump check is easier on a dry day.

Standard homeowner policies commonly exclude sewer backup and sump pump failure unless you added an endorsement. Read yours before the season starts.

What older Haverhill housing tends to show

Secondary census figures put Haverhill's median home at about 1964, with nearly four in ten built before 1940 and close to half before 1960. That age shapes what a plumber sees.

  • Galvanized or cast iron lines that narrow inside and clog repeatedly.
  • Old shutoff valves under sinks and at the main that stick or leak when turned.
  • Bathroom additions drained through a long, shallow run to the house line.
  • Mid-century basements with floor drains that tie into the main line.
  • Mismatched repairs, where copper meets older steel at a corroded joint.
  • Water heaters moved or replaced without updated relief piping.

A hidden drip behind these walls is where leak detection earns its keep, because it locates the wet spot before anyone opens the plaster.

Bradford, the Highlands and the rest of the city

Bradford was its own town until Haverhill annexed it on January 1, 1897, and the Bradford Common Historic District still anchors the south bank. Houses there around the common are old, so expect the age-related issues in the list above.

On the north side, downtown near the library at 99 Main St and the Highlands toward Winnekenni Park are the other long-settled areas. Ward Hill and Rocks Village sit further out, and a longer drive means we ask a few extra questions on the phone to bring the right parts the first time.

While the Truck Makes the Drive to Haverhill

Because we come from out of town, these minutes count for more here.

1

Stop the source

Turn the main shutoff clockwise. If it will not move, close the valve on the line nearest the leak and note which one failed.

2

If water or waste is coming up a drain, stop the washer, dishwasher and toilets. More flow into a blocked line makes the mess larger.

Raise cords, boxes and furniture legs out of the water. Do not walk through water that reaches an outlet or the electrical panel.

Keep the phone nearby, unlock the front door path, and tell dispatch about narrow driveways or a locked gate.

Lake water, lead notices and the line from the street

Haverhill's drinking water is all surface water. It comes from the Millvale Reservoir and Crystal Lake, pumped to Kenoza Lake, where a plant at 131 Amesbury Rd treats it. The 2025 report lists a pH of 7.6, lead not detected in the 2023 sampling, and copper at 0.018 ppm. The report does not state a hardness figure, so test your tap if scale concerns you.

In late 2025 the city began mailing annual notices about service line materials. A notice about lead, galvanized or unknown material does not mean the water is unsafe, but it may mean the line needs work. Call the city's line at 978-374-2368 for details, and confirm which part of the pipe you own before any dig. Our water line repair page explains the options.
